Replace legacy

Know what replacing your legacy system involves before you commit to it.

Fusion starts with a discovery: a free scoping session that works out exactly what your legacy system does, what it costs you to keep, and what replacing it would take. You leave with a written scope, a five-year cost baseline and a proposal priced from the savings — not an estimate and an invoice.

Total cost of ownership

A legacy system costs more than its licence.

Add up what the system has actually cost over five years and three lines dominate. Fusion removes two of them and shrinks the third.

Licences per user

Grows with headcount

Every person who logs in is a line on the renewal. The more of the business uses the system, the more it costs, and the harder it becomes to give access to the people who would benefit from it.

The implementation partner

Paid to configure, then paid to change

The partner who bent the product to fit your business is the partner you call for every change since. Their day rate is part of the system's cost, whether or not it appears on the same invoice.

The people who keep it running

Upgrades, workarounds, integrations

The in-house effort that keeps a system alive past the point where anyone would choose it: the upgrade nobody wants to run, the spreadsheet beside it, the integration that breaks each time either end changes.

50% lower five-year TCO, at most

A Fusion replacement runs on machines and a subscription that scales with what you run, not with who logs in. There is no licence per user, there is no implementation partner between you and a change, and the plumbing arrives as a dependency we maintain. Two of the three lines go, and the third shrinks to the few people who build.

Up to 50% is the top of the range, not a promise. The figure for your system is whatever your TCO baseline shows once the discovery has priced both sides over the same five years, and that figure is the one the proposal is built on.

Discovery

A free scoping session, with three things to show for it.

The discovery uses the same steps as every Fusion delivery. It costs you nothing, and it ends with documents you can act on whether or not you go ahead.

A ringed number is a step that needs people from the business. There are two of them.

  1. 01

    Sponsor session

    1 hour Business time

    Sit with the sponsor and agree which system is in scope, what it has to keep doing on day one, and what a good outcome looks like in the run costs.

    Session transcript
  2. 02

    Observation sessions

    1 hour each Business time

    The people who use the system today walk through the work it carries, including the parts that live in spreadsheets and inboxes around it. That is where the real scope hides.

    Observation notes
  3. 03

    Current state documented

    Write up the workflows and the system architecture as they stand, and pull together every cost line that touches the system: licences, partner days, hosting, and the people who keep it running.

  4. 04

    Domain model and value stream

    Design the model the replacement rests on, and the value stream its workflows make up between them. This is what turns a wish list into a scope with edges.

    Domain Model Workflows Value Stream
  5. 05

    Scope, baseline, proposal

    The three outputs, written and handed over. They are yours to keep, and they are the documents the deal is measured against if you go ahead.

    Written scope TCO baseline Value-based proposal

Written scope

What the replacement includes, what it leaves out, and what it must keep doing from the first day it is live.

TCO baseline

Five years of what you pay now, line by line, against five years of the replacement. Same period, same lines, so the two columns can be compared.

Value-based proposal

The share of the savings Fusion is paid, when it becomes payable, and what happens if the savings do not show.

The discovery is not the Readiness Assessment. That maps where your delivery fragments; this scopes one system's replacement and prices it.

Value-based

Paid from the savings, after they arrive.

There is no upfront build fee. Fusion is paid an agreed share of the TCO savings the replacement realises, and only once those savings show in your run costs against the baseline from the discovery.

You pay

  • An agreed share of realised savings

    The share is agreed in the proposal and measured against the baseline you signed off at discovery. It is invoiced after the saving appears in your run costs, not when it is forecast.

  • The Fusion subscription, at list price

    The environments you run and the people who build, exactly as on the pricing page. The value-based deal covers the replacement engagement; it does not change those lines.

You do not pay

  • A build fee up front

    The discovery is free, and the build is not invoiced as a project. Fusion carries the delivery cost until the savings are real.

  • A licence per user on the new system

    However many people use what replaces the legacy system, none of them is a seat. Widening access is not a pricing event.

  • For savings that never show

    The share is of realised savings. If a line in the baseline does not fall, nothing is due on it.

The proposal is a number you can check against your own run costs, every year, for the whole period.

How this sits with the pricing page

Delivery services are quoted per engagement, and this is the quote for a legacy replacement. The subscription lines stay as listed, the share applies only to the engagement, and the baseline that measures it is the one you signed off at discovery.
